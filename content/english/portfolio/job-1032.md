Some jobs go smoothly. This one did not — and it became the best thing I've built.

Job 1032 is a floor to ceiling built-in bookcase with base cabinets, a butcher block counter top, and arched upper shelving. On paper it's straightforward. In practice, it was one challenge after another — out of plumb walls, out of level floors, the kind of house that was built by humans and settled for decades before I showed up with a tape measure and expectations.

The arched upper sections were the centerpiece and the biggest headache. Getting consistent, smooth arches that matched each other across three bays required templates, patience, and more than a few do-overs. When they finally came together the way I had them in my mind's eye, it was worth every bit of it.

The base cabinets run the full width with inset doors — my standard. Inset because overlay is easier and I don't do things the easy way when there's a better way. The butcher block counter top ties the upper and lower sections together and gives the piece a warm, natural anchor point.

This job took longer than I estimated. Significantly longer. The client has been patient and gracious, and in return they got a piece built to a standard I wouldn't compromise on even when it cost me time I hadn't budgeted. There are a few things I'm still going back to address — not because they'd notice, but because I notice. That's the Tekton standard.

When it's fully complete and the final photos are taken, this will be the piece I show every prospective client first.
