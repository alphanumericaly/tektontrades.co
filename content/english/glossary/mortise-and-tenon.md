The 90's Toyota pickup of joinery — not flashy, just refuses to die. A tongue (the tenon) is cut on the end of one piece and fits into a matching pocket (the mortise) in the other. The wood itself carries the load, not a screw, and the long glue surfaces keep the joint from racking or working loose. It has held tables, chairs, and doors together for thousands of years. When a piece has to take daily abuse for decades, this is the joint doing the work.
