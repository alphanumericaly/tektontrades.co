Picture a dovetail stretched into a rail. One piece gets a flared tongue along its edge, the other a matching flared groove, and the two slide together like a drawer on a track. Once it's home, it cannot pull straight out — the shape locks it mechanically, with or without glue. On a table top, a dovetailed cross-piece underneath holds the top flat while still letting the wood swell and shrink with the seasons, so it won't cup or crack. On a shelf, it locks the shelf into the cabinet side for good — the shelf can't pull loose and the sides can't bow away from it. Slower to cut than a screw is to drive, and it will outlast every screw in the house.
